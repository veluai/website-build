# Case Study Summary

Internal source record for future website, Upwork, sales, proposal, presentation, and marketing use. Internally, identify the client as Homewoven. Public derivatives must use a generic description such as "Affordable Housing Nonprofit," "Mid-Sized Affordable Housing Nonprofit," or "Affordable Housing & Community Development Nonprofit" until disclosure approval is obtained.

Homewoven is an affordable-housing and community-development nonprofit that Velu served from July 2023 through May 2026. Velu took over the accounting and financial-management function from a long-tenured in-house Director of Finance.

The books were generally current and reconciled when Velu began. The central challenge was not a broken accounting system. The finance function was highly dependent on one individual, heavily paper-driven, difficult to transfer, limited in project- and grant-level visibility, and not designed for scalable cloud-based workflows or sufficiently transparent program-team financial participation.

Over approximately three years, Velu transformed the function into a more scalable, cloud-based, team-supported finance function with stronger controls, better project and grant visibility, structured management reporting, and a two-level outsourced finance team. The role evolved from hands-on implementation to outsourced Director-of-Finance / CFO-level leadership while a Velu accountant handled routine execution.

The client later chose to hire an in-house accountant and separately engage another outsourced controller/CFO provider. The engagement ended on good terms. Velu provided focused knowledge-transfer training to the incoming accountant; do not characterize this as a Velu-designed insourcing transition.

## Client Background

Homewoven is an affordable-housing nonprofit operating home repair, renovation, purchase-rehab-resale (PRR), new-construction, internship, fundraising, and related community-development programs.

At the beginning of Velu's engagement in July 2023:

- Operating budget was approximately $4.7 million.
- Actual 2023 expenses were approximately $3.96 million.
- Employee count was approximately 12.9 FTEs.
- The organization operated approximately six major departments/programs.
- Approximately 160 repair/renovation jobs were budgeted.
- Approximately 10 PRR housing projects were included in the budget.
- The organization had an established QuickBooks Desktop environment.
- Buildertrend was used for construction/project activity.

By 2026:

- Operating budget was approximately $7.5 million.
- Employee count was approximately 14 FTEs before adding outsourced accounting capacity.
- Approximately eight major departments/programs were tracked.
- Approximately 250 repair/renovation jobs were budgeted.
- Approximately 10 PRR homes were budgeted.
- The finance environment had become materially more complex.

Do not imply that Velu caused the organization's budget, program, or staffing growth. Preferred public framing: "Velu built and maintained finance infrastructure that supported the organization as its operating budget, program activity, and funding complexity increased."

## Starting Situation / Challenge

The accounting records and reconciliations were generally current when Velu began. The prior Finance Director had extensive institutional knowledge, but the finance function was heavily dependent on that individual.

Starting issues included:

- Key account access, coding decisions, grant information, project-funding decisions, vendor/payment processes, payroll processes, and historical accounting knowledge were concentrated with one person.
- Key accounts were often established under the former Finance Director's individual identity.
- The QuickBooks Desktop chart of accounts had too many categories, individual employee wage accounts, inconsistent parent/subaccount organization, duplicate or overlapping categories, older net-asset/equity presentation concepts, and overly detailed financial statements.
- Program Classes were directionally appropriate but did not provide sufficient individual project visibility for PRR and new-construction work.
- Restricted grants were tracked primarily outside the accounting system in spreadsheets instead of through a consistent internal grant-tracking structure.
- Buildertrend estimates/proposals were not routinely reconciled with the accounting system, limiting reliable project budget-to-actual reporting.
- Accounts payable was paper-based, with manual approvals and physical check signatures; coding often depended on institutional knowledge or in-person conversations.
- Seven credit and merchant accounts required manual reconciliation, and receipts were often delivered with limited explanation.
- Finance documentation was largely stored in filing cabinets, making historical research time-consuming and sometimes unsuccessful.
- The organization had a financial procedures manual but lacked detailed SOPs for recurring accounting work.
- Program teams had limited visibility into grant funding, project budgets, actual costs, remaining budget, funding-source decisions, and grant deadlines.

The core challenge was to turn person-dependent knowledge and paper-based work into scalable, cloud-based systems and repeatable processes while improving project, grant, and program-team financial visibility.

## What Velu Did

### Year 1 - Build & Implement Scalable Systems and Processes

Year 1 focused on learning, redesigning, implementing, documenting, and training. Tyler initially owned most of the finance function directly.

- Learned inherited processes through extensive knowledge transfer from the prior Finance Director.
- Developed SOP-style documentation for accounts payable, spend management, payroll, and recurring accounting processes. The detailed SOP library was maintained internally in Velu's practice-management system; do not imply a comprehensive client-facing SOP manual was formally delivered.
- Transitioned key systems so the Executive Director/organization was the authorized representative where appropriate and accounting staff had appropriate user access.
- Developed SOP-style access practices and annual access-review procedures; no formal organization-wide policy was established.
- Migrated QuickBooks Desktop to QuickBooks Online and modernized the chart of accounts, including improved nonprofit net-asset/equity structure.
- Expanded program and project class tracking for repair/renovation, PRR, and new-construction activity.
- Implemented grant-level accounting in the accounting system so grant expenses, overhead allocations, and reporting were more system-driven and required less spreadsheet reconstruction.
- Implemented BILL Accounts Payable and BILL Spend Management, including leadership, program-personnel, and vendor onboarding.
- Replaced manual/paper processes with electronic invoice retention, approval routing, payment tracking, and documentation.
- Reduced fragmented credit/merchant-account processes into a more controlled spend-management environment.
- Began moving finance records to electronic retention for accounts payable, spend management, payroll records, grant awards, grant agreements, and supporting accounting documentation.
- Established clearer project/grant coding responsibilities, trained program staff on financial information, and improved project-cost tracking while keeping day-to-day accounting and monthly reporting current.

### Year 2 - Delegate & Manage

As processes became standardized, recurring execution was progressively delegated to a Velu accountant. The outsourced structure became two-level: accountant-level execution and Director-of-Finance/CFO-level leadership from Tyler.

- The Velu accountant became the primary day-to-day resource for bookkeeping, accounts payable, spend-management administration, payroll-related processing, recurring transaction work, grant reimbursements, grant reporting, and preparation for monthly program/project reporting.
- Tyler reviewed work, managed the finance function, developed reporting, supported leadership, oversaw accounting infrastructure, led budgeting/forecasting, handled complex grant/project analysis, and supported Finance Committee and Board reporting.

### Year 3 - Lead & Advise

By Year 3, Tyler's role had shifted primarily toward outsourced Director-of-Finance/CFO-level leadership. Tyler reported directly to the Executive Director.

- Monthly budget vs. actual analysis and forecasting.
- Annual budgeting, grant budgeting, grant financial models, and project financial models.
- Finance Committee and Board reporting.
- Executive decision support, project/program financial oversight, and financial recommendations to leadership.
- Capital-campaign cash-flow analysis, banking/financing coordination, and audit-preparation oversight.

### Program, Project, and Grant Financial Management

- Established recurring financial-management meetings for repair/renovation, PRR, and later new-construction programs.
- Integrated Buildertrend with QuickBooks Online so bills could flow through Buildertrend to QBO to BILL and back, while program staff entered job-cost information in the project-management environment.
- Reconciled accounting to project reporting and used recurring meetings to review actual performance.
- For repair work, reporting included grant-level and project-level reporting, job counts, average cost per job, budget vs. actual, and remaining budget.
- For PRR and new construction, reporting included individual homes, grant sources, project budgets, actual costs, remaining budget, progress, time-to-completion KPIs, grant-period deadlines, and timing requirements.
- Educated program teams on grants, project budgets, allowable funding sources, financial performance, remaining budget, and project timing so they could participate in funding-source decisions, overruns, and operational adjustments.

### Activity / Job-Based Budgeting

- Redesigned annual budgeting around activity and jobs.
- For repair/renovation programs, budgets incorporated job count, average cost per job, expected program cost, and grant/funding assumptions.
- Produced budget views by major program, sub-program, project, and funding source where needed.
- Linked operational targets to financial planning in language program leaders could use.

## Accounting / Process Improvements

- Finance moved from highly person-dependent and paper-driven to a scalable, cloud-based operating model.
- The finance function became effectively paperless within approximately one year.
- Institutional knowledge was converted into documented workflows, systems, and repeatable processes.
- QuickBooks Desktop was migrated to QuickBooks Online and the chart of accounts was modernized.
- Grant accounting moved from spreadsheet-heavy external tracking toward integrated accounting-system tracking, including documentation of overhead allocations.
- Project accounting evolved into recurring project-level budget-to-actual reporting and KPIs.
- BILL Accounts Payable established electronic invoice retention, documented approval history, electronic payments, clearer project/grant coding, and stronger payment tracking.
- Paper checks were largely eliminated, except for certain escrow-related payments that could not be processed electronically.
- BILL Spend Management established individual employee cards, defined limits, program/personnel budgets, receipt-upload and description requirements, clearer coding, and easier reconciliation.
- Electronic accounts-payable workflows created greater transparency into invoice submission, approval, and payment timing.
- Cloud documentation improved audit trails, vendor communication, access practices, and the ability to provide services virtually.
- Board and Finance Committee reporting was professionalized and standardized, expanding on retained dashboard concepts rather than implying every original metric was created from scratch.

## Outcomes

- Leadership and program teams gained better visibility into grant funding, project budgets, actual costs, remaining budget, project timing, grant deadlines, and budget decisions.
- Program leaders became active participants in recurring financial discussions, raising questions proactively and using financial data to adjust operations.
- Finance became more integrated into operational decision-making.
- The organization gained scalable cloud finance infrastructure: QBO, integrated project accounting, cloud AP, controlled spend management, electronic approvals, electronic document retention, stronger access practices, clearer coding, and standardized reporting.
- The function was no longer dependent on one individual; it was supported by documented processes, cloud systems, program-team participation, accountant-level execution, CFO/Director-of-Finance leadership, and clearer organizational access.
- Finance became effectively paperless within approximately one year, enabling a transition from extensive onsite implementation to primarily virtual service delivery during the final approximately two years.
- Finance Committee and Board reporting became more professional, standardized, and decision-oriented.
- The function supported annual unmodified ("clean") audit opinions throughout the engagement, including two Single Audits with no findings.
- The organization had better financial support for capital-campaign cash-flow planning, potential financing discussions, and increasingly complex grant/project activity.
- When the organization later changed its finance model, Velu provided focused onboarding and knowledge-transfer support to the incoming accountant.
