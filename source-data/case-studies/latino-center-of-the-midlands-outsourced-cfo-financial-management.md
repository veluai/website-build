# Case Study Summary

Internal source record for a public nonprofit and flagship Tyler Wilcox Upwork portfolio case study. Latino Center of the Midlands (LCM) has worked with Velu for approximately seven years.

Velu originally replaced another outsourced accounting provider. The accounting records were generally current at the start of the relationship, but leadership was dissatisfied with the level of financial support. Leadership needed a trusted financial partner who could help interpret financial information, support management decision-making, and communicate effectively with the Finance Committee and Board. Fund accounting, budgeting, and related accounting processes also needed strengthening.

The engagement began as bookkeeping and financial-management support but quickly evolved into an outsourced CFO / finance director-level financial leadership role. Velu built and maintained financial-management infrastructure that supported LCM through significant organizational growth, increased funding complexity, stronger governance, and long-term sustainability planning.

During the relationship, LCM's annual operating budget grew from approximately $1 million to more than $4.2 million at its peak. Do not state or imply that Velu caused this growth. Preferred framing: Velu supported leadership and the finance function as organizational and funding complexity increased.

## Client Background

Latino Center of the Midlands is a nonprofit serving the Omaha community. Velu has served LCM for approximately seven years, and the relationship remains ongoing.

At the beginning of the engagement:

- LCM had an approximate $1 million annual operating budget.
- The organization had approximately 25 employees.
- There was no internal bookkeeper.
- Velu replaced another outsourced accounting provider.

Over time:

- Annual budget reached more than $4.2 million at peak.
- Staff grew to approximately 50.
- The restricted and government grant portfolio expanded significantly.
- Payroll and workforce complexity increased substantially.
- LCM expanded programs that required more sophisticated accounting, budgeting, grant reporting, and financial-management processes.

Staff growth and payroll are supporting indicators of organizational complexity, not the central public story.

## Starting Situation / Challenge

LCM leadership was dissatisfied with the prior outsourced accounting relationship.

- Leadership lacked confidence in the financial information.
- Management did not have a trusted financial advisor to help interpret the numbers and support decisions.
- There was no outsourced finance leader capable of confidently presenting financial information to the Finance Committee and Board.
- Financial management and financial planning were limited.
- Fund accounting needed to be stabilized and strengthened.
- Budgeting needed improvement.
- Some accounting cleanup may have been required, but the books must not be characterized as severely deficient without further documentation.

Recommended positioning: "The organization did not simply need bookkeeping. Leadership needed a trusted financial-management partner who could help them understand the numbers, make decisions, and communicate financial performance to the Finance Committee and Board."

## What Velu Did

Velu's work evolved from recurring accounting support into CFO/director-level financial leadership. Key work includes:

- Stabilizing fund accounting and strengthening accounting and reporting processes.
- Establishing stronger annual activity-based budgeting and multi-year financial planning when needed.
- Developing grant budgets, including budgets supporting change capital and new programs.
- Improving management reporting and supporting leadership decision-making.
- Leading the monthly Finance Committee process by developing agendas, preparing financial materials, presenting financial information, leading financial discussions, explaining performance and risks, supporting committee decision-making, and providing regular reporting to the Board.
- Building Finance Committee and Board reporting processes as organizational complexity increased.
- Managing increasingly complex restricted and government funding through fund and grant accounting, cost allocations, reimbursement requests, monitoring, compliance support, and grant-pipeline financial discussions.
- Providing or overseeing recurring bookkeeping, accounts payable, accounts receivable, invoicing, reconciliations, month-end close, financial statements, and year-end accounting.
- Implementing and improving cloud-based accounts-payable, bill-approval, spend-management, and financial-documentation processes.
- Evaluating payroll-system alternatives, developing and presenting a recommendation, supporting selection of a new payroll platform, and assisting with implementation and transition as workforce complexity increased.
- Preparing and maintaining accounting records for annual external audits, including year-end close, audit schedules, supporting documentation, auditor coordination, and accounting questions.
- Supporting auditor RFP and selection when applicable.
- Participating in financial planning and policy work supporting a broader consultant-led capitalization initiative: providing financial analysis and context, translating consultants' recommendations into operating financial policies, drafting and presenting policies to the Board, and supporting a Board-designated operating reserve, a working-capital line of credit, and a three-year operating budget / financial plan.

## Accounting / Process Improvements

- Established a recurring monthly financial-management cadence: monthly budget vs. actual analysis, monthly forecast vs. budget analysis, Finance Committee review, and Board financial reporting.
- Implemented activity-based annual budgeting that incorporates programs, staffing, expected revenue, grant funding, operating assumptions, and management priorities.
- Strengthened fund and grant accounting as restricted and government funding became more complex.
- Professionalized Finance Committee and Board reporting with financial dashboards and decision-oriented discussions.
- Improved liquidity reporting and visibility into restricted and unrestricted cash, supported by stronger fund accounting.
- Implemented cloud-based accounts-payable, bill-approval, spend-management, and documentation workflows.
- Supported payroll-system evaluation and implementation to meet greater organizational and workforce complexity.
- Built reliable accounting and reporting processes that supported annual external-audit preparation.

## Outcomes

Focus public outcomes on strategic financial-management improvements rather than routine accounting tasks.

- Leadership gained a trusted financial partner and greater confidence in the organization's financial information.
- A reliable recurring financial-management cadence was established.
- Management receives monthly budget-performance and year-end forecast analysis.
- Finance Committee and Board financial reporting became consistent, structured, and decision-oriented.
- The finance function scaled alongside significant organizational and funding complexity.
- LCM's annual operating budget grew from approximately $1 million to more than $4.2 million at peak during the relationship.
- Fund and grant accounting became capable of supporting greater restricted and government funding complexity.
- Leadership and the Board gained stronger visibility into liquidity and restricted versus unrestricted resources.
- Accounting records consistently supported annual unmodified ("clean") audit opinions.
- Financial systems evolved as organizational complexity increased.
- Recent work has supported liquidity planning, reserves, capitalization, and long-term sustainability.
- Velu remains LCM's outsourced financial-management partner after approximately seven years.
